## 57. About grades

**Date:** Fri, Dec 26, 2025, 3:27 PM ET · **Author:** Giacinto Paolo Saggese
**Link:** https://umd.instructure.com/courses/1391619/discussion_topics/5896810

Hello MSML610 crew,

I am addressing a few emails from students who noticed a mismatch between the ELMS grade and the final letter grade shown on UMEG.

**Why the grades differ**

We have reviewed all the grades after the break and the discrepancy comes from how grades are computed:

- **ELMS "Total"** — this is an equally weighted average of intermediate scores across assignments, quizzes, and projects.
- **Course grading policy** — according to the [grading policy](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1YXCrqh6KGg3xm4-Lr4QGdBnjeWEfkqz67FHNHB_rdAk/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.hd47oxflojxp), the final grade is weighted as:
  - 40% quizzes
  - 60% project

The final grades are then re-normalized to a Gaussian to account for whether the class was overall too easy or too difficult (aka "relative grading" or "grading on the curve"). This year, the projects were uniformly strong, with several being truly outstanding, which made the relative grading more noticeable.

It's also important to note that the grading scale was tilted in your favor:

- Extra points were awarded for completing the tutorial assignments and the survey (even though participation reached 74% rather than the 80% target)
- Projects were graded very generously

As a result, this class achieved the highest average grade among my classes in the past four years.

**A brief reflection**

I recognize that some of you may still feel you deserved a higher grade.

I know this feeling well, having experienced it many times myself. Even today as I was reading the news, I wonder why venture capitalists give [$8M](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/23/marissa-mayers-new-startup-dazzle-raises-8m-led-by-forerunners-kirsten-green/) to people after a string of evident failures, but not to me — someone who works relentlessly, even on Christmas (see my [GitHub commit history](https://github.com/gpsaggese)), and always delivers.

There will be many moments in life when outcomes feel unfair — jobs or opportunities you won't get because you're not from the "right" nation, background, color, religion, gender, or network.

My only advice is to bottle that feeling and turn it into fuel. Work so hard and become so good that you can't be ignored for long.

Despite my envy for all the hyper-achievers who got much more than what I deserve, I recognize that I've been extremely fortunate in many ways. So don't only look at those who got luckier than you, but also at those who got less lucky than you. On a more uplifting note, in the long run, the central theorem seems to hold: people tend to get what they deserve, plus or minus a small standard deviation.

Stay in touch, and good luck with college and life!

Best,
GP
