\section{System architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The canary subsystem spans two code directories and reuses the gateway of
\NoesisServer{}. Figure~\ref{fig:canary-architecture} shows the components and
the data that flows between them. The top two rows show offline calibration and
its result, the next two rows show online probing, and the bottom two rows show
the checker and its stored verdicts. The
remainder of this section describes the responsibilities, the persisted data
model, and the design principles.

\subsection{Components}
\label{sec:components}

Table~\ref{tab:modules} lists the modules, grouped by role. The
\texttt{checker/} directory holds both pure decision logic and the persistence
module; \texttt{prober/} is a thin coordination layer that imports scheduling,
grading, models, and persistence from \texttt{checker/}, and routing, providers,
faults, and request logging from the gateway.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.36\linewidth}X@{}}
    \toprule
    Module & Responsibility \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{checker\_models} & Immutable values shared by all checker modules
      (canary, grade, observation, policy, schedule item, verdict) \\
    \texttt{checker\_canaries} & Bank loading and validation; keyed,
      family-balanced selection \\
    \texttt{checker\_grading} & Versioned normalizers and exact-match grading \\
    \texttt{checker\_capability} & \texttt{CapabilityMeasurer} protocol and the
      deterministic default implementation \\
    \texttt{checker\_calibration} & Pure per-canary acceptance rule \\
    \texttt{checker\_calibration\_runtime} & Concurrent provider calls,
      summaries, and persistence of one run \\
    \texttt{checker\_calibration\_report} & Markdown findings for a completed
      run \\
    \texttt{checker\_probe\_schedule} & Keyed schedule and due-slot selection \\
    \texttt{checker\_evidence} & Classification of requests as eligible or
      excluded evidence \\
    \texttt{checker\_statistics} & Wilson and Clopper--Pearson upper bounds \\
    \texttt{checker\_verdicts} & Evidence minima, failure precedence, and
      verdicts \\
    \texttt{checker\_store} & All SQL: bank registration, calibration runs,
      probe slots, verdict finalization \\
    \texttt{prober\_runtime} & One non-blocking pass: claim, execute, grade,
      persist \\
    \texttt{prober\_worker} & Repeated passes with retry and graceful shutdown
      \\
    \texttt{prober\_cli} & \texttt{calibrate} and \texttt{probe} commands \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Modules of the canary subsystem.}
  \label{tab:modules}
\end{table}

\subsection{Persisted data model}
\label{sec:data_model}

All state lives in PostgreSQL (migration \texttt{002\_checker.sql}).
Table~\ref{tab:tables} lists the tables that the subsystem reads and writes.
Three integrity constraints carry much of the design.
First, the table \texttt{canary\_probes} has a unique key on
\texttt{(contract\_id, probe\_slot)}, which makes slot claiming idempotent.
Second, a check constraint on \texttt{requests} requires that a canary row has
a canary identifier and that a non-canary row has no canary identifier, no
grade, and no grader version, so that a grade cannot be attached to buyer
traffic. Third, \texttt{canary\_calibration\_results} is unique per
\texttt{(run, canary, role, provider, model, attempt)}, so a run cannot record
the same attempt twice.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.31\linewidth}X@{}}
    \toprule
    Table & Content \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{canaries} & One row per canary: prompt, answer, normalizer, family,
      generator seed, bank version, \texttt{status} (\texttt{DRAFT},
      \texttt{CALIBRATED}, \texttt{ACTIVE}, \texttt{RETIRED}), measured
      \texttt{healthy\_acc} and \texttt{weak\_acc}, and metadata that names the
      last calibration run \\
    \texttt{canary\_calibration\_runs} & One row per run: bank version,
      healthy and weak models, repetitions, state, configuration, summary \\
    \texttt{canary\_calibration\_results} & One row per attempt, including the
      raw response, provider status, correctness, latency, and cost \\
    \texttt{canary\_probes} & One row per claimed slot: state, linked request,
      grader version, error, scheduled and completed times \\
    \texttt{requests} & One row per request, with \texttt{is\_canary},
      \texttt{canary\_id}, \texttt{canary\_correct}, \texttt{grader\_version},
      pinned and actual provider, status, and latency \\
    \texttt{contract\_verdicts}, \texttt{verdict\_evidence} & The verdict, its
      counts and bounds, reason codes, and the per-request classification
      that produced it \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Tables read and written by the canary subsystem. The status
  \texttt{RETIRED} exists in the schema, and no code path sets it in the
  version described here.}
  \label{tab:tables}
\end{table}

\subsection{Design principles}
\label{sec:design_principles}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pure decision logic.} Acceptance, selection, scheduling,
    classification, and verdicts are pure functions of their inputs. Database
    access is confined to \texttt{checker\_store.py}, so replaying frozen
    evidence under the same policy version reproduces the same result.
  \item \textbf{No spend by default.} The \texttt{calibrate} command validates
    its inputs and prints the number of paid calls, and it contacts providers
    only when \texttt{--execute} is passed.
  \item \textbf{Constraints over coordination.} Concurrency control uses
    database uniqueness and guarded state transitions
    (\texttt{UPDATE ... WHERE state = ...}) rather than application locks.
  \item \textbf{Versioned artifacts.} The bank, the grader
    (\texttt{GRADER\_VERSION = m2-v1}), and the checker policy carry version
    strings that are stored with the data they produced.
  \item \textbf{No blame without attribution.} Every outcome has a
    classification that states whether it counts against the seller
    (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}).
\end{itemize}
